# Specialty catalog

A menu of seats and what each typically owns. Adapt the seat to the case, and invent a seat when none fits. Each entry lists what the seat usually owns and the signals that it belongs on the panel.

## Contents

- Organ-system specialties
- Diagnostic specialties
- Procedural specialties
- Cross-cutting specialties
- Life-stage specialties

## Organ-system specialties

**Cardiology.** Owns ECGs, echocardiograms, troponin and natriuretic peptide trends, rhythm, blood pressure, and heart-related drug effects. Signals: chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, swelling, abnormal ECG or echo, a new rhythm.

**Pulmonology and critical care.** Owns chest imaging, oxygen needs, blood gases, pulmonary function, pleural fluid, and ventilation decisions. Signals: breathing trouble, abnormal chest imaging, low oxygen, ICU care.

**Gastroenterology and hepatology.** Owns liver panels and their pattern, synthetic function, GI bleeding, pancreas, bowel imaging, endoscopy findings, and liver biopsy decisions. Signals: abnormal liver tests, jaundice, abdominal pain, bleeding, ascites.

**Nephrology.** Owns creatinine and eGFR trends, electrolytes, acid-base balance, urine studies, fluid balance, dialysis decisions, and drug dosing for kidney function. Signals: rising creatinine, abnormal sodium or potassium, low urine output.

**Neurology.** Owns the neurological exam over time, brain and spine imaging, EEG, nerve studies, spinal fluid, and mental status. Signals: confusion, weakness, seizures, headache, gait change, numbness.

**Hematology.** Owns blood counts and smears, clotting studies, bleeding and clotting risk, transfusion, bone marrow, and blood cancers. Signals: abnormal counts, clots, bleeding, abnormal clotting tests, lymph node or spleen enlargement.

**Oncology.** Owns the malignant branch: suspicious imaging, tumor markers and their limits, tissue results, staging, paraneoplastic syndromes, and which tissue target gives the best yield for the risk. Signals: a mass or lesion, unexplained weight loss, abnormal markers, cancer history, a pathology report.

**Infectious disease.** Owns the pathogen differential, cultures and molecular tests, exposure history, antimicrobial regimens as actually given, drug interactions and toxicity monitoring, and what has and has not been excluded. Signals: fever, rising inflammatory markers, positive cultures, travel or exposure history, immune suppression.

**Rheumatology and immunology.** Owns autoimmune serologies and their limits, inflammatory markers, vasculitis, granulomatous disease, immune deficiency, and steroid and immune-therapy effects on testing. Signals: joint, skin, or multi-organ involvement, positive autoantibodies, unexplained inflammation.

**Endocrinology.** Owns glucose control, thyroid, adrenal, pituitary, calcium and bone, and hormone-producing tumors. Signals: abnormal glucose, sodium, or calcium, weight change, steroid exposure, adrenal or pituitary findings.

**Dermatology.** Owns rashes, skin lesions, and skin biopsies as clues to systemic disease. Signals: new rash, skin changes with systemic illness.

**Urology and gynecology.** Owns the urinary tract, prostate, and pelvic organs, their imaging, and procedures. Signals: urinary findings, pelvic masses, related symptoms.

## Diagnostic specialties

**Radiology.** Owns every imaging study as a sequence: what each study added, disagreements between reads, protocol limits (contrast phase, sequence, field of view), exam time against order time, and what a different modality could show. Signals: more than a few studies, conflicting reads, an indeterminate finding.

**Pathology and laboratory medicine.** Owns tissue, cytology, and flow cytometry reports and the exact wording of their certainty categories, specimen adequacy, lab comments, assay limits, interference, and sample handling. Signals: any tissue or fluid result, a lab deviation comment, a surprising lab value.

**Medical genetics.** Owns inherited and late-onset genetic explanations, family history patterns, and genetic testing strategy. Signals: suggestive family history, early onset, multi-system disease with no explanation.

## Procedural specialties

**Interventional radiology.** Owns image-guided biopsy and drainage options: which target, which route, expected yield, complication risk, and what the procedure would and would not settle. Signals: a biopsy or drainage under consideration, a hard-to-reach lesion.

**General or specialty surgery.** Owns surgical diagnosis and treatment options, operative risk, and open biopsy. Signals: a surgical abdomen, a resectable lesion, a procedure under discussion.

**Neurosurgery and orthopedics.** Owns spine, brain, and bone structural problems and procedures. Signals: compression, fractures, structural lesions.

## Cross-cutting specialties

**Clinical pharmacology or clinical pharmacist.** Owns every drug on the list: interactions, doses against kidney and liver function, side effects that mimic disease, drugs that suppress test sensitivity, and what was ordered versus given. Signals: many medications, a new drug before a change, organ dysfunction affecting dosing.

**Toxicology.** Owns ingestions, supplements, environmental and occupational toxins, and drug-induced syndromes. Signals: supplements or herbal products, occupational exposure, an unexplained syndrome.

**Palliative care.** Owns symptom burden, goals of care, and making sure decisions reflect the patient's values. Signals: serious illness, hard decisions ahead, distressing symptoms.

**Psychiatry.** Owns mood, behavior, delirium versus primary psychiatric illness, and psychiatric drug effects. Signals: behavior change, psychiatric history, psychoactive medications.

**Physical medicine and rehabilitation.** Owns function, therapy measurements, and discharge readiness. Signals: loss of function, disposition questions.

## Life-stage specialties

**Geriatrics.** Owns frailty, delirium risk, falls, medication burden in older adults, and baseline function. Often folded into the clinical generalist.

**Pediatrics and its subspecialties.** Replace adult seats with pediatric ones for a child. Growth, development, and age-specific ranges matter.

**Obstetrics and maternal-fetal medicine.** Owns pregnancy and postpartum physiology and pregnancy-specific conditions. Signals: current or recent pregnancy.
