# Interview guide

Topics in order. Each has its purpose, starter questions, and follow-ups worth asking when an answer opens a door. Rephrase naturally. Never read the list aloud as a form.

## Contents

## 1. Current situation and timeline

Purpose: the story of this illness in order, from the first sign to today.

- When did you first notice something was off? What was it?
- What happened next, and roughly when?
- What led to the hospital or the current workup?

Follow-ups: symptoms that came and went, anything tried at home, earlier visits for the same problem, what made things better or worse, and the first thing that seemed different from the patient's normal.

## 2. Past medical history

Purpose: conditions that could explain, mimic, or complicate the current problem.

- What health conditions has the patient been diagnosed with, even minor or long ago?
- Anything that was being watched or never fully explained?

Follow-ups: childhood illnesses, pregnancies and complications, mental health history, old abnormal test results someone mentioned, and conditions resolved years ago.

## 3. Surgeries and procedures

- Any operations, procedures, implants, transfusions, or hospital stays before this one? About when?

Follow-ups: complications, hardware or devices, organ removals, and procedures done abroad.

## 4. Medications and supplements

Purpose: the full list, including what the chart often misses.

- What prescription medications was the patient taking before this started? Any recently started, stopped, or changed?
- Any over-the-counter medicines taken regularly, such as pain relievers, sleep aids, or antacids?
- Vitamins, supplements, herbal products, teas, protein powders, or products bought online?

Follow-ups: dose and how often, when each started, anything borrowed from someone else, recreational substances, and alcohol amount and pattern. Ask without judgment and explain that doctors need it to read the tests.

## 5. Allergies and reactions

- Any allergies or bad reactions to medicines, foods, contrast dye, latex, or anything else? What happened?

## 6. Family history

Purpose: inherited risk and conditions that run in families.

- Among parents, brothers and sisters, children, and grandparents, what serious illnesses have there been? At what age?
- Any cancers, heart problems, clotting problems, autoimmune diseases, liver or kidney disease, neurological conditions, or early deaths?

Follow-ups: ancestry when relevant to inherited conditions, relatives with a similar illness, and genetic testing anyone has had.

## 7. Social history and exposures

Purpose: environmental, occupational, and travel clues that widen the differential.

- Where does the patient live, and what kind of home? Well water or city water?
- What work have they done, now and in the past? Any exposure to chemicals, dust, metals, fumes, or animals at work?
- Any travel in the past year, inside or outside the country?
- Pets, farm animals, hunting, fishing, gardening, or outdoor hobbies?
- Tobacco or vaping, now or before?

Follow-ups: tick or insect bites, unusual foods, sick contacts, recent vaccinations, and time spent in a region with endemic infections.

## 8. Baseline function before this episode

Purpose: what normal looked like, so change can be measured.

- Before this started, how was the patient doing day to day: walking, working, driving, managing money and medications, memory, mood, appetite, and sleep?
- Any change in weight over the past year?

Follow-ups: devices used such as a cane or hearing aids, and a typical day.

## 9. Earlier workups and outside records

- Has the patient seen other doctors or had tests for this problem, or for anything related, before this episode?
- Are there records, imaging discs, or results from other hospitals that this care team may not have?

Follow-ups: where and when. Suggest adding copies to `Other Records/`.

## 10. The care team as the family knows it

- Who has the family met, and in what role? Who seems to be in charge?
- What has the team said so far about what they think is going on and what comes next?

Record what was said and by whom, with dates. This is family report of verbal updates, not the chart.

## 11. What the family most wants to understand, and what worries them

- What are the biggest questions you want answered?
- What worries you most?
- Is anything important to the patient that the team should know, such as values, wishes, or fears?

This section steers later analysis and question lists. Record it in the family's own words.
